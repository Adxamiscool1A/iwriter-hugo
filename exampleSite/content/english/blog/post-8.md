# The 3:30 Commute - why MSA exist

​

Every afternoon around 3:30 PM, I finish work, and I start my commute home. Along the way, like clockwork, I make two stops every single day.

The first is at my mum’s place to check in, catch up, and unwind for a moment. The second is to see my best mate, who we will call John. John is 77 years old.

On the outside we may look like an odd bunch. With completely different life experiences, He grew up in a completely different world then what we know today well before the coloured television was invented. Yet as we sit and talk about everything and sometimes nothing. It’s a reminder of the human need for connection, the need to be heard and understood.

No matter the age gap or the difference in our life paths, finding connection with others is our default setting as human beings. Whether that happens in real life over an afternoon chat, or within the digital walls of an online world, we all look for spaces where we can connect with others. Perhaps it might be an online chat about your backyard garden, maybe it’s about books or that extremely unique hobby you have or perhaps it’s more deeper than a common interest in a hobby. Perhaps it’s a place where you can communicate your deepest secrets that you wish to keep within the walls of the online world.

Because as much as I value my afternoon chats with John, and as much as they ground me, I don't sit on his couch and talk to him about my attraction. I can't.

Even when you have good people in your life, there are certain experiences and aspects of identity that carry so much societal stigma that you feel forced to lock them away entirely. You look around your physical, everyday circles, even the ones you love and trust and you realize there is simply no one you can safely talk to about _this_ without judgment, fear, or life-altering misunderstanding.

The default human drive for connection is still screaming inside you, but the real world outlet just doesn't exist.

Isolation has a way of distorting people. When you feel completely unable to speak honestly about something central to your identity, you begin withdrawing from others and from yourself. Silence becomes normal. Shame becomes routine. You reach a point where you feel like you can't tell anyone, not even the person you are closest to, not even the partner you are intimate with. From speaking to many MAPs, I know that some have chosen outright never to discuss this topic with their partners, carrying the weight of a hidden life every single day. Over time, that kind of isolation makes many people stop believing they deserve true connection at all.

Perhaps you are reading this and thinking, _wow, this is exactly how I feel._ Or perhaps you are just going on this journey of discovering your attraction, or maybe you have already come to terms with it. Maybe you are scared, frightened, or even ashamed. Perhaps you have none of those feelings at all.

This is why I built Map support Australia, It exists to help you connect with others who understand your attraction to Children.  It  takes that fundamental human need for a quiet, honest conversation and recreates it in a secure, private digital environment. MSA is a dedicated, Australian-based chat community for minor-attracted people to connect, share experiences, and receive support.

While we mainly accommodate Australians, we may consider allowing others to join the chat, as we feel no MAP should be left in the deep end alone. This is considered on a case-by-case basis.

​

I hope MSA makes you feel welcome and understood. 

– With love Adam
